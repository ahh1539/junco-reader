Junco Reader vs Quick TTS

Both run text-to-speech entirely in your browser with no account and no upload. Here's where they actually differ, as of this writing.

  Junco Reader quick-tts.com
Cost Free, no ads Free, ad-supported (Google AdSense)
Instant voice Yes -- your browser's built-in voice, no download Yes -- Web Speech API by default
Neural voice engines Kokoro-82M (on-device) Kokoro-82M, Piper, and Supertonic (three engines)
File formats PDF, TXT, Markdown, pasted text PDF, DOCX, EPUB, ODT, RTF, HTML, TXT, Markdown, plus OCR for scanned PDFs
Documents leave your device Never Never
Installable (PWA) Yes Not offered as far as we can tell
Companion app Junco for iPhone -- newsletters as a daily podcast None

Where quick-tts.com is ahead

It reads more file formats -- DOCX, EPUB, ODT, RTF, and HTML on top of PDF -- and it can OCR a scanned (image-only) PDF, which Junco Reader can't do yet. If you need one of those formats today, quick-tts.com is the better fit. It also offers two extra neural engines (Piper and Supertonic) alongside Kokoro, for more voice variety.

Where Junco Reader is ahead

No ads. Junco Reader is built by the team behind Junco, an iPhone app that turns your email newsletters into a short daily podcast -- if you like the on-device Kokoro voice here, it's the same voice family reading your inbox out loud every morning. Junco Reader is also installable as an app (Add to Home Screen), and reads PDFs and Markdown files directly without needing an account.

Try it yourself -- drop in a PDF or paste an article, and switch to the Instant tier if you don't want to wait on a download.
